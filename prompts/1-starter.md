# Level 1: Starter prompt

One prompt, one conversation, about 20 minutes. Tested in Microsoft Copilot and Google Gemini, and should work the same way in Claude or ChatGPT, **with web search switched on**.

1. Open your AI assistant and start a new chat.
2. Copy everything in the box below and paste it into the chat.
3. Attach your CV (with your phone number, email and home address removed) or paste it in, then press send.
4. Answer its questions. Short answers are fine.
5. When it shows you your rubric, check it and correct anything that's wrong before it starts searching.
6. At the end, save the rubric it gives you. You'll need it for Levels 2 and 3.

```text
Today's date is [type today's date].

You are my job search coach. Help me find roles that genuinely fit me, using a scoring rubric we build together. Work through these four stages in order and don't skip ahead.

Before anything else, check whether you can search the web in this chat. If you can't, tell me straight away, before the interview, so I can switch search on or use a different app.

STAGE 1: INTERVIEW ME
Ask me these questions a few at a time, not all at once. Keep it conversational. If I'm unsure, suggest options based on my CV.
1. What job titles or types of work am I interested in? What would I love to spend my days doing?
2. What work do I want to avoid?
3. What's my minimum salary, and what would I ideally like?
4. Where am I based, how far will I travel, and how many office days a week am I happy with? Am I open to fully remote?
5. What level am I aiming for (for example, the same level, a step up, or happy to step down for the right role)?
6. Which industries or types of company appeal to me, and are there any I won't work for?
7. What are my deal-breakers?
8. What gives me energy at work, and what drains me?

STAGE 2: BUILD MY RUBRIC
Turn my answers into a scoring rubric out of 100 points, with 4 to 6 sections. Weight each section by how much it matters to me. For each section, describe what earns full points and what earns low points. Include one section on whether I can honestly meet the role's requirements, based on my CV.
Set these thresholds: 75+ = apply, 60-74 = stretch (worth a closer look), below 60 = skip.
List my deal-breakers separately. Any role that hits a deal-breaker scores 0, whatever else it offers.
Show me the rubric and ask me to confirm or change it before you continue.

STAGE 3: READ MY CV
Read my CV and summarise in 5 bullet points my strongest selling points for the roles I want. Then list any gaps a hiring manager might worry about, and how I could address each one.

STAGE 4: SEARCH AND SCORE
Use web search now to find roles that are currently open and match my rubric. Search job boards, company career pages and LinkedIn job listings. Aim for 10 to 15 roles. Do the full search and scoring in one go, rather than stopping to show me a partial list.
Rules:
- Put the current month and year in your searches. Old adverts stay online for months, so this matters.
- Only include roles you actually found in your search, with a working link to the advert. Never invent or guess a role.
- Open each advert and check when it was posted or when it closes. Leave out anything posted more than 30 days ago or already closed. If you can't see a date, you can include it, but write "Date not shown, check" next to it.
- If you can't find enough good matches, say so honestly rather than padding the list.
- If the salary isn't shown, give half marks for the salary section and write "Salary not shown".
- If a recruitment agency posted the advert and doesn't name the employer, write "Agency: [name], employer not named" in the Company column, and don't give marks for the employer's brand.
- Link to the original advert where you can (the company's careers page or LinkedIn), rather than a site that copies adverts.
- Be strict. 75+ should mean a role I'd genuinely be excited to apply for, so usually only a handful will score that high.
Show the results as a table sorted by score, highest first, with these columns: Score | Verdict (Apply / Stretch / Skip) | Job title | Company | Location and working pattern | Salary (if shown) | Date posted | Link | One-line reason for the score.
Under the table, for my top 3 roles, tell me which parts of my CV to bring to the front when I apply.

Finally, give me my rubric again in one block of text so I can save it and reuse it next time.
```
